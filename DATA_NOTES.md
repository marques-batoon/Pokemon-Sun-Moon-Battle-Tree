# Battle Tree data notes (Pokémon Sun & Moon)

How the Sun/Moon Battle Tree picks opponents, as reconstructed from the sources, and where this app has to guess. The data lives in `src/data/battle-tree/` and is generated by `npm run data:build` from the raw extracts in `data-sources/battle-tree/` (see `SOURCES.md` there). Every JSON file carries `source` keys that point into `sources.json`.

Confidence labels used below:

- **Datamined**: read out of the game files.
- **Wiki**: stated by Bulbapedia, with no contradicting source.
- **Community-tested**: measured by players in-game; not read from code.
- **Approximation**: no source covers it; the app picks a reasonable rule and says so.

## 1. Pokémon sets — `sets.json` (datamined)

- 996 sets, the Sun/Moon v1.1 list. Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon has 999 (Raticate-1, Arbok-1 and Golbat-1 were added) and changed 15 existing sets. None of the USUM changes are applied here.
- Each set fixes species, nature, held item, up to 4 moves and EVs. Two invested stats get 252 each, three get 170 each, and the one four-stat set gets 127 each.
- Four Sun/Moon sets have fewer than 4 moves: Aerodactyl-1, Rhyperior-2, Porygon-Z-2 and Serperior-4. They are kept as-is.
- Sets 204 (Kommo-o-4) and 226 (Pidgeot-1) changed in the v1.1 patch. The app uses the v1.1 versions.
- Cross-check: Bulbapedia's list (SM rows) and Level 51's sheet agree with the Team Rocket Elite dump on all 996 sets, field for field.

## 2. Trainers — `trainers.json`

| Ids | Count | What |
|---|---|---|
| 0–189 | 190 | Regular trainers (Bulbapedia #001–190) |
| 190 / 191 | 2 | Red (Super Singles boss) / Blue (Super Doubles boss) |
| 192–202 | 11 | Special trainers |
| 203 / 204 | 2 | Red (Normal Singles boss) / Blue (Normal Doubles boss) |

- **Rosters are datamined.** Each trainer draws from a fixed list of set ids. They were cross-checked against SadisticMystic's USUM rosters plus that sheet's Sun/Moon differences. There was one mismatch: Kendra's Heat Rotom-1, which the sheet's notes leave out. Tree-Sets puts Kendra and Rocky in the same pool, so the dump is right.
- **Names, classes and order** match Bulbapedia for all 190 regular trainers.
- **IVs (community-tested):** every Pokémon a trainer uses has the same IV in all six stats:
  - Trainers #001–050: 19
  - Trainers #051–070: 23
  - Trainers #071–090: 27
  - Trainers #091–190, special trainers and Battle Legends: 31

  This comes from atsync's in-game testing, which the author called an educated guess. SadisticMystic's IV-tier formula gives the same tiers. The game does not tie IVs to the battle number: a 31-IV special trainer can appear at battle 10.

## 3. Choosing the opponent — `brackets.json`

### Normal Singles (wiki)

A run lasts 20 battles and ends after the 20th.

| Battle | Opponent |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | Uniform random from trainers #001–050 |
| 11–19 | Uniform random from trainers #031–070 |
| 20 | Red, Normal team (trainer 203) |

Beating Red unlocks Super Singles. There are no special trainers in Normal. Normal and Super share trainer pools and IVs: Bulbapedia's table makes no Normal/Super split, and atsync's tests found Normal uses the same trainers as the first 20 Super battles. (In the Gen 6 Battle Maison, Normal had its own weaker trainers. That does not carry over to the Tree.)

### Super Singles (wiki)

Super has no length limit; a run ends only on a loss. For battle *N*, the first matching rule wins:

1. **N = 50:** Red, Super team (trainer 190).
2. **N is a multiple of 10 (10, 20, 30, 40, 60, 70, …):** a special trainer (see below).
3. **Otherwise, uniform random from a bracket pool:**

| Battles | Trainer pool | Pool size |
|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | #001–050 | 50 |
| 11–19 | #031–070 | 40 |
| 21–29 | #051–090 | 40 |
| 31–39 | #071–110 | 40 |
| 41–49 | #091–130 | 40 |
| 51+ | #091–190 | 100 |

Bulbapedia states that trainers within a range have equal weight. SadisticMystic's per-trainer min/max battle numbers give the same ranges.

### Special trainers (wiki)

| Version | Pool |
|---|---|
| Both | Anabel, Colress, Cynthia, Grimsley, Wally |
| Sun only | Kiawe, Plumeria, Sina |
| Moon only | Dexio, Guzma, Mallow |

Kukui is Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon only and is left out.

- **Weights:** each version's pool has 8 trainers. Anabel appears 1/7 as often as each of the others, which gives weights of 1 against 7 (Anabel 1/50, each other trainer 7/50).
- **No version choice in the app:** a special battle draws as if the version were picked at random each time. Trainers in both versions keep their weight; each version-exclusive trainer gets half of it (3.5/50), so Plumeria and Guzma, Kiawe and Mallow, and Sina and Dexio are equally likely, and each pair together is as likely as one of them would be in its own version. Anabel stays at 1/50 and the four other shared trainers at 7/50 each. **App choice** (the game fixes the version).
- **Anabel's condition:** she can only appear once the player has reached Guzzlord's chapter of the Looker episode. Before that, Bulbapedia says a random ordinary trainer battles instead.

## 4. Building the opponent's team (community-documented)

From Smogon's mechanics guide:

1. Pick sets from the trainer's roster uniformly at random, one at a time.
2. Reject a pick that would repeat a species or a held item (the AI follows Species Clause and Item Clause).
3. Stop when the team is full: 3 in Singles.

A tested property: every one of the 205 rosters can field a clause-legal team of 4.

- **Level:** 50.
- **Ability:** uniform over the three ability slots [1, 2, Hidden]. An empty slot repeats ability 1, so a species with one ability plus a Hidden Ability rolls 2/3 normal, 1/3 hidden. The AI can roll Hidden Abilities that weren't released at the time (Oranguru, Passimian, Heatran). Showdown's special `S` slot (e.g. Battle Bond) is never rolled.
- **Gender:** random.
- **Bosses:** Red picks 3 of his 10 sets with the same rule.

## 5. Player rules — `rules.json` (wiki)

- **Bringing 3 (community guide):** you enter with a registered team and pick 3 for Singles. You keep those 3, in the same order, for every battle until you take a break or lose. There is no Team Preview: the opponent's Pokémon are only seen when sent out. After a break you may change your team, and the streak continues. The app follows this by default (approved 2026-10-03); a run setting turns on Showdown-style Team Preview before every battle instead.

- **Level:** Pokémon above Lv. 50 drop to 50 for the battle. Pokémon below 50 are **not** raised.
- **Species Clause and Item Clause** apply to the whole registered team (up to 6), not only the 3 brought.
- **Banned species**, including all forms: Mewtwo, Mew, Lugia, Ho-Oh, Celebi, Kyogre, Groudon, Rayquaza, Jirachi, Deoxys, Dialga, Palkia, Giratina, Phione, Manaphy, Darkrai, Shaymin, Arceus, Victini, Reshiram, Zekrom, Kyurem, Keldeo, Meloetta, Genesect, Xerneas, Yveltal, Zygarde, Diancie, Hoopa, Volcanion, Cosmog, Cosmoem, Solgaleo, Lunala, Necrozma, Magearna, Marshadow, Zeraora. Tapus and Ultra Beasts are allowed.
- **Between battles:** every battle starts fresh, with full HP and PP, no status, and consumed items back.

**Battle Points per win:**

| Battles | Normal | Super |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | 1 | 2 |
| 11–19 | 2 | 3 |
| 20 | 20 (Red) | 3 |
| 21–30 | — | 4 |
| 31–40 | — | 5 |
| 41–49 | — | 6 |
| 50 | — | 50 (Red) |
| 51+ | — | 7 |

Smogon's guide calls the 20 and 50 a "bonus". Bulbapedia's table lists them as the whole award for those battles. The app follows Bulbapedia.

## 6. Where the app deviates or guesses

| Topic | What the app does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Anabel locked | The replacement trainer comes from the bracket of battle N−1 (battle 20 → the 11–19 pool) | The source doesn't say which bracket. **Approximation** |
| Looker progress | Exposed as a run setting (Anabel unlocked yes/no) | The game reads this from your save |
| Game version | Not chosen: Sun- and Moon-exclusive special trainers share the exclusive slots with equal odds (section 3) | Requested; the game fixes the version. **App choice** |
| Repeat opponents | Allowed, independent draws every battle | No anti-repeat rule is documented anywhere. **Approximation** |
| Team pick and ability odds | As in section 4 | From the community guide, not code. **Community-documented** |
| Gender | Rolled with the species' gender ratio | The ratio the game uses isn't documented. **Approximation** |
| Opponent lead | First Pokémon picked | Not documented. **Approximation** |
| Gourgeist size | Average | No source gives a size, and the dump labels every other non-default form. **Approximation** |
| IVs | Table in section 2 | **Community-tested** |
| Unreleased Hidden Abilities on AI Pokémon | Allowed; AI teams skip the legality validator | Matches the game |
| Team Preview | Off by default (game rule); optional per-run setting shows both teams and picks 3 every battle | Spec asked for preview; the game has none. Toggle approved 2026-10-03 |
| Next opponent shown early | The run screen shows the next trainer's class and name before you choose to battle, save or change team | Requested by the spec. In the game the trainer appears when the battle starts, so this is extra information |
| When the opponent is rolled | Battle N's trainer and team are derived from the run seed when battle N−1 ends, so saving and reloading doesn't reroll them | Same odds as rolling at battle start; makes runs reproducible |
| Interrupted battle (app closed mid-battle) | You choose: count it as a loss (what the game does) or replay the battle from the start with the same opponent and seed | Replaying isn't possible in the game |
| Retire | Ends the run; the streak is recorded | The game ends the streak on loss or retreat |
| Debug runs | "Start at battle N" and "Unlock Super" exist for testing; such runs never count toward records or the Super unlock | Testing aid |

## 7. Source errors found and resolved

- **Regional forms on 4 special trainers.** Bulbapedia's trainer page gives Colress the Kantonian Muk-1/Muk-2, Dexio Ninetales-2, Kiawe Marowak-2 and Sina Sandslash-2, in addition to the Alolan sets. The Team Rocket Elite dump, SadisticMystic's sheet and Tree-Sets all list the Alolan sets only. The app follows the three.
- **Rotom sprites.** Bulbapedia's Pokémon list shows the Fan and Frost Rotom sprites swapped on the "-3" sets. The moves (Air Slash vs. Blizzard) confirm the dump's labels. This is a display error only.
- **Kendra's roster.** SadisticMystic's Sun/Moon notes omit Kendra's Heat Rotom-1. The dump and Tree-Sets include it (see section 2).
- **Opponent IVs.** An early draft of Smogon's guide said opponents had 11 IVs. That was corrected in the same thread to start at 19.

## 8. Player legality decisions (approved 2026-10-03)

The sources leave these open, so they're product decisions rather than facts about the game:

- **Learnsets:** Showdown's Gen 7 learnsets, which merge Sun/Moon with Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon move tutors. Exact Sun/Moon-only move legality isn't reliably derivable from them.
- **Species:** limited to Sun/Moon. Poipole, Naganadel, Stakataka, Blacephalon, Zeraora and Dusk Form Lycanroc are banned (`USUM_ONLY_SPECIES` in `src/engine/format.ts`).
- **Held items:** every Gen 7 item is allowed.
- **Run settings:** each run has an "Anabel unlocked" toggle (default on). There is no Sun/Moon choice; version-exclusive special trainers from both games can appear (section 3).
- **Levels:** the builder defaults to Lv. 50 and allows lower levels. In battle, anything above 50 is lowered to 50. Showdown applies this rule inside its team validator, so `applyFlatRules()` enforces it explicitly before every battle.
- **Zero EVs:** Showdown's validator complains about a set with 0 EVs; that warning is ignored because 0 EVs is legal in the Battle Tree.

## 9. App-level choices (Phase 6)

- **Practice runs:** challenges against the random AI are unranked. Like debug runs, they never count toward records, BP or the Super unlock.
- **Images:** the original spec asked for placeholder visuals only. That was changed on request: Pokémon sprites and trainer portraits now load from Pokémon Showdown's sprite server (not bundled; © Nintendo / Creatures / GAME FREAK). They can be switched off in Settings. Trainer class → sprite mapping, including the approximations where Showdown has no Sun & Moon art (e.g. one Golfer sprite for both genders), is in `src/data/battle-tree/trainer-sprites.json`.
- **Opponent card:** the next trainer is shown big and centred. Regular trainers get their Showdown sprite at 3× size (2× on phones), scaled with crisp pixels. Official artwork is drawn 25% taller than that sprite (300px, 200px on phones). Battles keep the small sprite.
  - **Artwork (special trainers and Battle Legends only):** 8 of the 13 have official art, loaded from PidgiWiki's image server (not bundled; © Nintendo / Creatures / GAME FREAK): Red, Blue, Plumeria, Guzma, Kiawe and Mallow (Sun & Moon art), plus Cynthia (Diamond and Pearl) and Wally (Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire), whose Sun & Moon designs match that art. Entries and reasons are in `src/data/battle-tree/trainer-art.json`.
  - **No artwork:** Grimsley, Anabel, Colress, Sina and Dexio get the big sprite. Their Sun & Moon designs have no official art that other sites can show: Grimsley's is only on the Bulbagarden Archives, which blocks embedding, and the others only have art of older outfits or concept sketches.
  - **Regular trainers have no artwork:** Sun & Moon has none for its trainer classes, only in-game 3D renders, which are hosted where embedding is blocked. The Pokémon Wiki (Fandom) also blocks requests from other sites, so it isn't used.
  - `node scripts/check-trainer-sprites.mjs` checks that every artwork URL still loads from another site.
- **Trainer quotes (written for this app):** every named trainer (190 regular, 11 special trainers, Red and Blue) has **three sets** of lines. Each set is a greeting plus two closing remarks, one for winning and one for losing, that call back to that greeting. These are **not** the games' dialogue: the real Battle Tree lines are Nintendo's script, so they aren't copied here.
  - **No Pokémon, types or team hints:** the lines never name a Pokémon (wild ones and ride Pokémon included) or a type, and never hint at what a trainer brings. Each trainer has their own personality instead, drawn from their class, their sprite and life in Alola. For example, Youngster Florian is a beachcomber collecting shells, Golfer Moe barely speaks, and Collector Duncan always has his arms crossed. A test fails if any line names a Pokémon or says "X-type".
  - **Special trainers and Battle Legends:** they speak in character. Red only says "…", with a stage direction.
  - **Choosing a set:** one set is picked per battle from the battle's seed, so the card, the intro and the closing remark always match, and a replayed battle gets the same lines.
  - **Where they appear:** the greeting shows on the opponent card and in the special-battle intro. The closing remark shows at the end of the battle (none on a tie). Free Battle uses Red's or Blue's.
  - **Data:** `src/data/battle-tree/trainer-quotes.json`.
- **Animations:** presentation only. Playback timing never changes battle outcomes, which come from the seeded simulator.

## 10. Pokémon Champions Megas (added on request, 2026-10-03)

The player can use the Mega Evolutions introduced after Sun & Moon (Legends: Z-A and its Mega Dimension DLC) that are in **Pokémon Champions** (through Regulation Set M-C), with their Mega Stones. Battle Tree opponents never use them; their teams are the datamined Sun/Moon sets.

- **List and data:** `src/data/champions/megas.json`: 39 Megas (40 simulator formes: Meowstic's Mega exists for male and female in Showdown's data, with the same Champions entry), with types, Ability, base stats and Mega Stone from Serebii's Champions Pokédex and item list. Not in Champions, so not added: Mega Heatran, Darkrai, Zygarde, Magearna, Zeraora and Tatsugiri.
- **Champions vs Legends: Z-A:** Champions changed five Abilities. The app uses Champions': Mega Absol Z **Sharpness** (Z-A: Magic Bounce), Mega Garchomp Z **Levitate** (Sand Force), Mega Lucario Z **Aura Guard** (Adaptability), Mega Golisopod **Tough Claws** (Emergency Exit), Mega Baxcalibur **Thermal Exchange** only (Thermal Exchange / Ice Body). Types and base stats match Showdown's Z-A data.
- **Aura Guard** (new in Champions, not in the simulator): the holder takes half damage from moves that make contact; Long Reach contact moves deal regular damage (Serebii, Bulbapedia). Implemented with the simulator's contact check, which also treats the attacker's Protective Pads as no contact. That Protective Pads interaction isn't documented. **Approximation.**
- **Mega Feraligatr's stats:** Serebii's page shows two different orders for Sp. Atk and Speed. op.gg and Showdown agree on Sp. Atk 89 / Speed 78, which the app uses. **Source conflict, resolved by majority.**
- **Pokémon that aren't in Sun & Moon** (approved): Falinks (Gen 8), Scovillain, Glimmora and Baxcalibur (Gen 9) are legal, with their Gen 9 learnsets, including Gen 8-9 moves (e.g. No Retreat, Spicy Extract, Mortal Spin, Glaive Rush). Eternal Flower Floette (needed for Floettite; event-only in Gen 6-7) is legal with its full learnset (Light of Ruin). Those newer moves stay off-limits to every other Pokémon, Smeargle's Sketch included. The generated list is in `src/data/champions/derived.json` (`scripts/build-champions-data.mjs`).
- **Engine:** the Battle Tree formats run on a Gen 7 mod (`gen7battletree`) that adds these entries; Gen 8-9 moves and Abilities use Showdown's implementations inside the Gen 7 engine. **Approximation** where a newer mechanic touches something Gen 7 lacks (e.g. Snowscape's snow weather).
- **AI:** @smogon/calc's Gen 7 data doesn't have these Pokémon or moves, so the AI passes their types, stats and move data from the simulator. The calc's Gen 7 mechanics don't model the new Abilities (Dragonize, Mega Sol, Piercing Drill, etc.) in damage estimates. **Approximation.**
- **Sprites:** probed on Pokémon Showdown's sprite server (`derived.json`). Every new forme has a front image (animated for most; static only for Mega Absol Z, Garchomp Z, Lucario Z, Golisopod and Baxcalibur). Where no back sprite exists (many of them), the player's side shows the front sprite mirrored.

## 11. Double Battles (added on request, 2026-10-03)

- **Rules:** register 4-6 Pokémon, bring 4 (Bulbapedia: "four for Double Battles"); the first two lead. Same banlist, Species Clause, Item Clause and level rule as Singles.
- **Schedule:** `brackets.json` `doubles`. Same trainer pools as Singles (Bulbapedia's trainer list has one table for every format). Battle Legend **Blue** at battle 20 (Normal) and 50 (Super) ("Blue is the Battle Legend for Double Battles and uses four Pokémon"). Special trainers every 10th battle in Super ("In Super Battles, special Trainers appear every ten battles"). Beating Blue in Normal Doubles unlocks Super Doubles.
- **Opponents bring 4** (regular, special and Blue), drawn with the same Species/Item Clause roster rule as Singles.
- **Blue's sets:** trainers 204 (Normal) and 191 (Super) from the Team Rocket Elite datamine. All 34 Sun/Moon sets match Bulbapedia's Battle Legend tables.
- **BP:** Bulbapedia's BP table is by win streak only, with no format column, so Doubles uses the same BP as Singles.
- **Records:** kept per format and course (`singles-normal`, `singles-super`, `doubles-normal`, `doubles-super`). Progress saved before Doubles existed is migrated: the old Super unlock becomes the Super Singles unlock.
- **AI:** see AI_NOTES.md "Double Battles" (an approximation; the Doubles AI isn't documented).

## 12. Custom additions (made for this app; not in any game)

Designed by the user and added on request (2026-10-03). Data in `src/data/custom/index.ts`, battle logic in `src/engine/custom.ts`. Battle Tree opponents never use them.

- **Politoedite / Mega Politoed** (`Politoed-Mega`): only Politoed can Mega Evolve with it. Water type like Politoed, Ability **Rain Dish**. Base stats are Politoed's with Sp. Atk +40, Defense +30 and Sp. Def +30: 90 / 75 / 105 / 130 / 130 / 70 (total 500 → 600, like other Megas). (Revised 2026-10-04; first version was Swift Swim with Sp. Atk +50, Def +20, Sp. Def +20, Speed +10.) Uses Politoed's sprites, drawn 20% larger as a Mega.
- **Poliwrathium Z:** only Poliwrath can use it. A damaging move of one of three types becomes that type's custom Z-Move, with the power and category the matching crystal would give (Fightinium, Waterium or Icium Z: power from the base move's Z power):
  - Fighting → **Omega Wrath**; afterwards Poliwrath sets up a Substitute without losing HP, and its Attack and Defense rise by 1.
  - Water → **Riptide Rocket Rush**; afterwards Attack +1, Speed +2.
  - Ice → **Glacial Guardian Gauntlet**; afterwards Attack, Defense and Sp. Def +1.
  - Moves of other types and status moves can't become Z-Moves with it. The after-effects happen only if the attack hits.
- **Poliwrath learns Drain Punch** (added 2026-10-04): in the games it only gets Drain Punch from Gen 8 on (TM), so its Sun & Moon learnset doesn't have it. Allowed for Poliwrath only (not Poliwag, Poliwhirl or Politoed). The list is `CUSTOM_LEARNS` in `src/data/custom/index.ts`.
- **Field turn counters** (display aid; the game shows none): weather, terrain, rooms (Trick Room, Magic Room, Wonder Room, Gravity) and Tailwind show "turn/total", e.g. "Rain 2/5". The total is the effect's real length from the simulator, including Damp Rock, Heat Rock, Smooth Rock, Icy Rock and Terrain Extender (8 turns). So an opponent's "/8" reveals that item, which the game wouldn't.
